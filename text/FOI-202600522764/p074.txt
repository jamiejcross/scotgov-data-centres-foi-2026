Hi [Redacted - S.38(1)(b)] – thanks for coming back, some interesting questions (now in
green) with our responses below:



The Charter states; “Data centres require backup generation to ensure 24/7 availability
during outages, typically diesel, but used infrequently”.



To set the scene, all of the standards are currently set by hyperscalers at a global level
to ensure that they are designing their systems to minimise any risk of downtime to their
customers - such as ScotGov or Apatura – and every other business and individual using
their services.



The requirement for backup generation for example is the same in the UK (where the
grid is very resilient and there is very little risk of disruption) as in somewhere like India
where outages at a grid level are much more common.



      What are industry standards for relying on backup generation notionally in GB? Is
       there a percentage of downtime annually that can be extrapolated?
      At GB transmission level (including Scotland), true loss-of-supply events under
       an N-1 secure network are extremely rare, and when translated to a “downtime
       equivalent,” they are e ectively measured in seconds to a few minutes per year
       (≪0.001%)


      What are industry standards for maintenance operation of backup generation
       (e.g. monthly for X minutes) of full system?
      Industry standard is that generators are selectively tested on a schedule similar
       to:
           o Monthly Testing: A basic operational test at 50% load for one hour
           o Quarterly Testing: A load bank test at 100% load for four hours
           o Annual Testing: A full diagnostic test at 100% load for four hours,
      Our ﬁndings through independent reports are that on this basis there are no
       signiﬁcant air quality impacts, either on human health or ecology.
